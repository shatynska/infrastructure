## Why

Recovering a host from nothing is a sequence that exists entirely — every workflow, every secret, every manual step — and that nobody has ever run end to end. It is recorded as one paragraph of prose in `docs/bootstrap-a-new-host.md`'s Appendix B, which names the stages to repeat but states no order a reader can follow without holding the whole bootstrap document in their head, names no credential per step, and gives no duration. A procedure whose steps are known and whose *sequence* is untested is a procedure that will first be attempted on the day it is needed, which is the worst day to discover that a step's precondition is not met.

That the prose has already drifted from the tree is the evidence, not a suspicion. Appendix B states that staging's data volume "is **not** wiped by a rebuild" — of a rebuild the same paragraph defines as *either* toggling `server_enabled` *or* replacing the server. `terraform/stacks/main-staging/main.tf` gives the volume `count = var.volume_enabled && var.server_enabled ? 1 : 0`: that count turns on the toggles and not on the server's identity, so the sentence is false of the toggle route and is plausibly true of the replace route. What it is not is a statement about "a rebuild", and a reader who takes it as one — most consequentially on production, where a forced replacement is the case that arrives unannounced — is reading a route-specific answer as a general one. A plan against live state settles which route does what; the rehearsal settles the *sequence*, which is the larger unknown and the one nothing can answer on paper.

`docs/backlog.md` `write-and-rehearse-the-rebuild-runbook` says what the deliverable is and which half matters: "The rehearsal is the point; the document is how it survives." Staging is where a rehearsal can happen without touching production — it has been converged since 2026-09-10 and has carried the platform stack since 2026-09-13, so the thing this entry once waited on is in place.

## What Changes

- **A new `docs/runbook-rebuild.md`**: the ordered steps to rebuild a host of this repository from a destroyed server to a serving one. Each step names the credential it needs and where that credential lives, what it changes, and how to tell it worked. It covers both stacks, taking `main-staging` as the worked example because that is the one the rehearsal runs against, and it names the production-only divergences rather than leaving a reader to infer them.
- **A rehearsal record inside that document**, in labelled lines and in exactly one of three states: never rehearsed, partially rehearsed, or rehearsed with its date, duration and stack. The document merges in the first of those, so the state a reader most needs to distinguish — written but never exercised — is stated outright rather than left as a blank field. The record is part of the runbook rather than a change artifact, because a runbook whose rehearsal state lives elsewhere is one nobody checks before following it.
- **An actual rehearsal**: `main-staging` is destroyed and rebuilt by following the document, and every step the run finds wrong is corrected *in the document* rather than annotated around.
- **`docs/bootstrap-a-new-host.md` Appendix B is reduced to a pointer** at the new document. Two accounts of one procedure drift, and the drift above is what that costs. The bootstrap document keeps what only it can say — that a rebuild's first converge is a workstation converge, and why — and hands the sequence over.
- **The volume/server coupling answer is scoped to the route it was observed on** and written where it is read — in the runbook, in the corrected Appendix B sentence, and in the dated note below. This is the first live observation of a coupling `docs/backlog.md` `exercise-the-volume-server-coupling-against-live-state` records as never exercised; it observes staging's toggle route, not production's and not the replace route, so that entry gains a dated note rather than being closed.
- **The alarms a rebuild raises are named as steps.** The stack's dead-man's-switch goes overdue minutes after the server is destroyed and pages for the whole window; the runbook says so before the step that destroys it, and carries the observer phase owed afterwards — naming the observer, and re-reading each check against the settings `docs/bootstrap-a-new-host.md` Appendix A records. The runbook cites that register rather than copying it, because `README.md` says those values are listed once and a copied lookup is what goes stale.
- **The register gains the block it is missing.** `README.md` states that the checks and their periods and graces "are listed once, in `docs/bootstrap-a-new-host.md`'s Appendix A". That claim is not true: Appendix A's table covers the prune checks and explicitly excludes the per-stack Alertmanager checks, whose period and grace are stated instead in §7.1 — five minutes each, beside the instruction to create the check — in a stage a rebuild's reader has no reason to open. So the values exist and the register does not hold them, which is the single-source claim failing rather than a missing value. Appendix A gains a second block addressed by `PLATFORM_DEADMANSWITCH_URL`, carrying what §7.1 already states, and §7.1 keeps the creation step and cites the register for the settings. The rows do not go in the ping key's table, whose count and rotation scope a nearby secret-inventory row states and which those rows would falsify.
- **What the runbook cites rather than copies is decided by the same line.** The database provisioning recipe stays in `docs/onboard-an-application.md`, where *Single Shared PostgreSQL Instance, Per-Application Databases* (`openspec/specs/iac-platform-services/spec.md`) confines it and a required check enforces it; `platform/README.md` keeps its two manual steps. The runbook carries what is sequence — that each is owed, at which point, and with which secret.
- **A `.github/tests` module**, derived from the delta specification by an author other than the implementer, asserting statically what the document must keep saying: that every step names a credential or states it needs none, that the rehearsal record is in exactly one of its three defined states, that its cross-references resolve, and that Appendix B holds no second copy of the sequence.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `iac-server-lifecycle`: adds a requirement that the procedure for rebuilding a host is recorded as an ordered, credential-naming document and that the document carries the date and duration of its last rehearsal — the operational counterpart of the toggle this capability already specifies, which today specifies that a server *can* be recreated and says nothing about everything that has to follow before the host serves again.

## Impact

- **New**: `docs/runbook-rebuild.md`; one module under `.github/tests/`, derived from the delta by `ai-toolkit:change-test-writer`, with the `test-plan.md` that dispatch produces.
- **Modified**: `docs/bootstrap-a-new-host.md` (Appendix B, its opening section, the §6.4 and §7.4 prose that points into it, **Appendix A**, which gains a second register block for the per-stack Alertmanager checks' period and grace, and the "Not in this table" paragraph that becomes its pointer — while the ping key's own table, its four-check count and its rotation-scope sentence are left untouched and true; and **§7.1**, which keeps the creation step and cites the register for the settings it currently states); `README.md` (its "listed once" paragraph, which is false today and must be made true of both blocks); `platform/README.md` (its one reference to Appendix B); `.github/workflows/platform-deploy.yml` (the comment on its `workflow_dispatch` naming Appendix B as the case it exists for); `docs/backlog.md` (this entry deleted at archive, `exercise-the-volume-server-coupling-against-live-state` gains a dated note, a new entry for the maintenance window that cannot announce a rebuild, and **two entries whose prose points into Appendix B** — `close-public-ssh-and-manage-sshd-explicitly` and `hold-every-platform-deploy-for-the-length-of-a-window`, both found by the sweep rather than anticipated, and added here rather than fixed silently). That list is what the repository-wide sweep for "Appendix B" is checked against — the sweep confirms a known list rather than discovering one.
- **Touched twice and reverted by the rehearsal**: `terraform/stacks/main-staging/terraform.tfvars` — `server_enabled` to `false` and back to `true`, one merge each. `main-staging`'s GitHub Environment requires no reviewer and its `pipeline.yml` sets `destroy_policy_gate: false`, so each merge applies immediately and unattended. No production stack file is edited at any point.
- **Live effect**: the `main-staging` host is destroyed and rebuilt. Its dead-man's-switch check `main-staging-alertmanager` goes overdue within minutes of the destroy and pages `#alerts` until the platform stack is back, and `main-staging-prune-host-images` is red from the rebuild until an activation is triggered by hand. Both are expected, both are named in the runbook before the step that causes them, and neither is muted. Its public IPv4 changes, its data volume and every store on it is lost, and `commerce-ops.main-staging.fincci.bike` is down for the duration. Prometheus history and Grafana's UI-created state on that host do not come back; nothing else on it needs a restore, under *No Store on This Host Holds Data Requiring Backup* (`openspec/specs/iac-safety-hardening/spec.md`). Production is untouched.
- **Outside this repository**: `commerce-ops`'s staging deploy is re-run from that application's own Actions, and its `SHARED_POSTGRES_PASSWORD` in its `staging` Environment is rotated by the re-provisioning recipe. No code there changes.
